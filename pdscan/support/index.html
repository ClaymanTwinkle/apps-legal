PDScan Support

PDScan measures your pupillary distance (PD) for ordering glasses online, with the Face ID depth sensor on your iPhone.

Contact

Email claymantwinkle@gmail.com. If a measurement goes wrong, open Settings in the app and tap Report a problem: it fills in the app version, device and your recent readings (no name, no images), and you can read all of it before sending.

Getting a good reading

  • Take your glasses off and face a light, so your eyes are evenly lit.
  • Hold the phone about 30 cm (a foot) from your face and look at the front camera, not the screen.
  • Keep your head level. The prompt at the top tells you to move closer, move back or lift or lower your chin.
  • If the result says Unsteady, measure again. For progressive lenses or a strong prescription, measure two or three times and use the median.

Which number goes where

  • One PD box on the order form: use your distance PD.
  • Two boxes (right and left, or OD and OS): use the PD for each eye. Right is OD, left is OS.
  • Reading glasses: use your near PD. A prescription that says 64/60 means distance/near, not right/left.

My result differs from my prescription

PD on prescriptions is often rounded to the nearest 0.5 or 1 mm, and opticians measure it in different ways, so a small difference is normal. Settings > How it works explains the method and the accuracy we saw in our tests. PDScan is not a medical device. If your prescription includes prism, or you have strabismus or another binocular condition, have your PD measured by an optician.

No Face ID on my device

iPhone SE, iPhone Duo and other devices without the Face ID depth sensor cannot do the depth measurement. PDScan offers an estimate instead: you take a selfie with any credit-card-sized card on your brow for scale and drag markers onto your pupils and the card. Typical error is 1 to 2 mm.
